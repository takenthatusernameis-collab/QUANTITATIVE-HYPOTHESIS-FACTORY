## Withdrawn — IDEA-001 RECON-FLOW (76.0 at rejection) — *Cycle-13 rejection (R-011); Cycle-1 rank 1*

**Cycle-13 verdict — REJECTED.** The dossier below is preserved unaltered as the audit trail; the
decision is in `rejected_and_parked.md` (R-011) and the summary is here. Three published results
close the candidate:
1. **The effect has collapsed (S-146, *Journal of Finance* 2025).** The S&P 500 addition abnormal
   return has fallen from 7.4% in the 1990s to **less than 1% over the past decade**; deletions
   average 0.1% between 2010 and 2020; the average inclusion effect in 2020 was **−3 basis points**.
   There is no concession of tradeable size left in the modern sample — the thing the candidate
   harvests no longer exists at a measurable magnitude.
2. **The differentiator has a published null (S-146).** "There is no apparent relationship between
   net purchases [by index trackers] and the index inclusion effect, for either additions or
   deletions." The candidate's stated differentiator was that the announcement-to-effective drift
   *scales with the required passive flow*. That is now a published negative.
3. **The mechanism is published and points elsewhere (S-147, with S-151).** Speculators act as
   *anticipatory liquidity providers* around reconstitution; their pre-positioning "shifts price
   pressure away from the reconstitution date"; "apparent front-running reflects competitive
   liquidity provision and benefits index investors"; short interest **rises on implementation dates
   and falls afterwards** as speculators cover. The candidate's chain required the concession to be
   paid *between the announcement and the effective close* and to persist until the arbitrageur
   unwinds. The published structure is anticipation *before* the event and unwinding *after* it —
   and S-151 explains why nothing is left to harvest: the counterparty to a passive fund's demand is
   **the issuing firm itself**, and most demand-side institutions trade in the same direction as
   index funds, so there is no arbitrageur paying a concession to capture.
**Two gaps closed in the candidate's favour in the same cycle, which is the point of the ordering:**
G-03 (the effective-close convention is documented by the index manager itself, S-149, and by
peer-reviewed work, S-150) and G-02/G-07 (a published method for estimating benchmark-specific
tracked assets exists, S-148). The candidate therefore died on **evidence**, not on an unresolved
premise — and the two gap closures are retained as reusable research assets for the register.
Its validation specification **V-001 is withdrawn**.

- **Mechanism, causal coherence, persistence (4/5):** The causal chain from provider rules (S-008)
  to scheduled non-price-sensitive demand to a liquidity concession to a partial reversal is
  explicit, and each link is documented by a different body of work. Not 5 because the *differentiated*
  links (flow scaling, capacity asymmetry) are conjectures rather than theory.
- **Quality and independence of supporting evidence (4/5):** Four independent scholarly lines
  (S-001, S-002, S-003, S-004) plus option-market evidence (S-005, S-006, S-007) and primary venue
  rules (S-008). Not 5 because the option-market evidence includes a working paper by authors
  affiliated with the index provider (S-006), and the classic samples predate today's passive scale.
- **Substantive novelty (2/5):** The underlying effect is forty years old and well published.
  What is claimed as new is a conditional, flow-scaled, two-leg specification. This is the lowest
  score in the register and it is deliberately visible: presenting this as a new edge would be false.
- **Clarity of falsifiable predictions and feasibility of a clean test (5/5):** Event dates are
  published in advance by the index provider; the design has no look-ahead risk on the event
  schedule; the flow-size prediction is quantitative; the kill conditions are explicit.
- **Resilience to costs, capacity, financing, adverse selection, regime (3/5):** Per-event capacity
  is small by construction, the reversal leg trades against the same crowd, and the premise depends
  on a trading convention (trade at the effective close) that is not publicly guaranteed (G-03).
- **Data availability, simplicity, reproducibility (5/5):** Public announcements, public index
  weights, public prices. The one missing input — assets tracking the benchmark — is a real but
  addressable gap (G-02, G-07).

10. **A published effect can be true and the register's version of it false, because the quantity is
    convention-dependent (new in Cycle 30).** IDEA-003's gate claims that in an imminent-crash state
    the variance risk premium "collapses" and short-volatility exposure becomes uncompensated. The
    2026 preprint that frames the premium as crash-conditional and "not harvestable carry" (S-181)
    is not a refutation of that claim — it measures the premium as implied minus *subsequently
    realized* volatility, which mechanically **widens** in stress because fear over-prices volatility
    that then mean-reverts, whereas the candidate's claim is about the **expected** premium. The two
    quantities can move in opposite directions in the same state, so a candidate whose prediction is
    stated in convention-free language is **untestable in principle**, and a null obtained under one
    convention does not kill a claim made under the other. This is now a standing requirement, added
    to the two existing screens: **every future dossier that conditions on a measured spread or
    premium must state the measurement convention ex ante, and every validation specification must
    fix it before the holdout is opened.** V-003 has been amended accordingly in Cycle 30.

12. **A slow state can still carry a daily rule, if the identification is cross-sectional (new in
    Cycle 35).** IDEA-010's capital state refreshes only when an AP files — quarterly, with a 40–90 day
    lag (S-225, S-226) — which looked fatal against a 1–10 session rule. It is not. A state that is
    constant between filings is fully usable for a claim of the form *"the level* of the state governs
    *the size* of an event-driven move", provided the identification is a **cross-sectional contrast
    between units with different states** rather than a within-unit time-series reading of state
    changes. Three conditions follow, and they generalise to every candidate whose state is a slow
    institutional quantity (IDEA-004's 13F crowd state is the other live case). (i) **The state is a
    filter over a cross-section; the event is what times the trade.** The flow, not the state, is the
    trigger. (ii) **The within-unit time-series reading is demoted to an ablation** and must be
    reported with its own effective-sample count, because the number of state *changes* per unit is
    small. (iii) **The effective sample must be counted in units of (state observation, event) rather
    than in sessions**, and "untested" must remain a permitted verdict when that count is too small —
    a null in a sample of one episode is not a kill. The general lesson: **a quarterly state is not a
    quarterly trade.** The error is to assume that a conditioning variable's refresh frequency must
    equal the holding horizon, which confuses the *conditioning variable* with the *trigger*. The same
    correction retires the Cycle-34 amendment's conditional instruction to extend the horizon or park
    IDEA-010 if the cadence proved infeasible: the cadence is feasible, the horizon is preserved, and
    the price of the resolution is a small effective sample that must be reported rather than
    glossed.
13. **A decision-time state must satisfy three tests, and the Cycle-52 screen closed the third (new in
    Cycle 52).** Until Cycle 52 the register's state requirements were two: the state must be **published
    before the decision** (G-13) and it must **determine the sign or the existence** of the effect
    (G-12). The R-032 venue-halt screen added a third, from an independent body of evidence: a state that
    is **broadcast in real time cannot carry an edge even when it is published and even when it is
    sign-carrying in principle**, because it is a public record of the flow it is supposed to predict
    (the LULD price bands are republished to the public every 30 seconds, S-260, and the EU regime
    *mandates* publication of trading halts, S-261). The three tests are therefore: **(i) published before
    the decision; (ii) sign-determining rather than merely informative; (iii) not a public real-time
    by-product of the flow it is meant to predict.** Two of the register's own candidates show what each
    failure looks like. **IDEA-014 fails (ii)** — the sign-determining RIN state publishes only *after* the
    deadline, and the published pre-deadline proxy is set against the agency's own supply projection, so
