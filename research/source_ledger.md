Status vocabulary used in dossiers:
- **Primary theory** — model or formal argument supporting the mechanism.
- **Prior empirical result (author-reported)** — the authors' own reported evidence. Not reproduced here.
- **Venue documentation** — primary rules/regulations/methodology documents.
- **Independent replication** — a separate team re-deriving the earlier result. None are claimed here.

### S-001 — Harris, L. and Gurel, E. (1986)
"Price and Volume Effects Associated with Changes in the S&P 500 List: New Evidence for the
Existence of Price Pressures." *The Journal of Finance* 41(4): 885–897.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1986.tb04550.x
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the price-pressure hypothesis — non-informational index-driven demand pushes prices
  above equilibrium and the effect partially unwinds; documents the announcement-day price and volume
  pattern around S&P 500 constituent changes.
- **Caveats:** small event sample; early-era index-fund scale; the magnitude of every price-pressure
  effect it estimates scales with the size of index-linked assets, which has grown by orders of
  magnitude since 1986.
- **Verification:** Wiley journal landing page (title, authors, abstract, date).

### S-002 — Beneish, M. D. and Whaley, R. E. (1996)
"An Anatomy of the 'S&P Game': The Effects of Changing the Rules." *The Journal of Finance* 51(5):
1909–1930.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1996.tb05231.x
Full text mirror used for reading: https://repec.udesa.edu.ar/pub/Finanzas/Journals/Journal%20of%20Finance/51/5/2329543.pdf
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the five-day pre-announcement regime and the "S&P game" — risk arbitrageurs buy after
  the announcement and sell into the index funds' effective-date demand; part of the price increase
  reverses after inclusion; also documents the overnight/intraday split of the effect.
- **Caveats:** sample covers 1986–1994 with a materially different index-fund asset base and a
  different index-provider rule set.
- **Verification:** publisher landing page (DOI) plus the full-text PDF.

### S-003 — Chen, H., Noronha, G. and Singal, V. (2004)
"The Price Response to S&P 500 Index Additions and Deletions: Evidence of Asymmetry and a New
Explanation." *The Journal of Finance* 59(4).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00683.x
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the *asymmetry* — permanent price increase for additions, no permanent decline for
  deletions — and the investor-awareness explanation offered for it.
- **Caveats:** awareness is proposed, not identified; the deletion side is confounded by index funds
  that must sell into a one-sided order book.
- **Verification:** Wiley journal landing page (title, authors, abstract).

### S-004 — Woglom, G. (2002)
"Index Inclusion Effects on Stock Prices: Is There Any Evidence of a 'Holdings Effect'?" Federal
Reserve Board FEDS Paper 2002-48.
URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2002/200248/200248pap.pdf
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** a model separating trading effects from fundamental effects on S&P 500 additions —
  an announcement price increase, a rise in post-addition volatility, and a growing fraction of the
  announcement gain reversed in subsequent weeks.
- **Caveats:** paper, not a journal article at the time of retrieval; reversal is partly attributed to
  a higher risk-adjusted discount rate rather than pure price pressure.
- **Verification:** Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis PDF (author, date, abstract, results text).

### S-005 — Chen, Y., Koutsantony, C., Truong, C. and Veeraraghavan, M. (2013)
"Stock price response to S&P 500 index inclusions: Do options listings and options trading volume
matter?" *Journal of International Financial Markets, Institutions and Money* 23: 379–401.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intfin.2012.09.008
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** announcement abnormal returns for 344 S&P 500 inclusions, 1996–2010, and the finding
  that *abnormal options trading volume before* the announcement is associated with smaller
  announcement abnormal returns (i.e., options markets pre-price the event).
- **Caveats:** association, not a mechanism test; sample restricted to S&P 500 additions.
- **Verification:** ScienceDirect landing page and EconPapers record (title, authors, venue, DOI).

### S-006 — Dash, S. and Liu, B. (2008)
"Capturing the Index Effect via Options." SSRN Working Paper 1321725.
URL: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1321725
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported), non-refereed.
- **Supports:** the index effect is large in the *options* of affected stocks — at-the-money call
  premiums of 20–30× the corresponding stock price move, with a larger effect for additions from
  outside the S&P 1500 family; documents announcement-to-effective option returns.
- **Caveats:** both authors were affiliated with S&P at the time (stated in the paper), so treat as
  interested-party evidence; a working paper, not peer reviewed; reported strategy returns are the
  authors' own and are not reproduced here.
- **Verification:** SSRN abstract page (title, authors, date, abstract).

### S-007 — Hollstein, F. and Simen, C. W. (2021)
"The Index Effect: Evidence from the Option Market." Auckland University Finance and Research
Working Paper Series.
URL: https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/530750/Announcement_paper.pdf
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported), working paper.
- **Supports:** delta-hedged option positions also respond to inclusion/exclusion news; the inclusion
  effect is permanent while the exclusion effect is transitory; the equity response is stronger for
  "outsider" additions (moving in from outside the S&P 400) than for intra-family promotions.
- **Caveats:** working paper; the authors' own test design (placebo and risk adjustment) is not
  independently audited.
- **Verification:** University of Auckland repository PDF (title, authors, date, abstract).

### S-008 — S&P Dow Jones Indices (venue documentation)
"S&P U.S. Indices Methodology" (index maintenance, timing of changes, announcements, share freeze,
rebalancing).
URL: https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/methodology-sp-us-indices.pdf
- **Type:** Venue documentation (primary rules).
- **Supports:** the institutional clock the mechanism depends on — constituent changes are typically
  announced **one to five days** before implementation; S&P 500 / MidCap 400 / SmallCap 600
  announcements are published at **05:15 PM ET**; changes take effect **after the close on the third
  Friday** of the quarter-end month; a "share freeze" applies in the week before the effective date.
- **Caveats:** the methodology is revised by the provider at its discretion; a specific event's rules
  must be read from the announcement itself and the version of the methodology in force at that date.
- **Verification:** provider-hosted PDF read directly (sections: Index Maintenance, Timing of Changes,
  Announcements, Rebalancing, Share Freeze).

### S-009 — Brunnermeier, M. K. and Pedersen, L. H. (2009)
"Market Liquidity and Funding Liquidity." *The Review of Financial Studies* 22(6): 2201–2238.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhn098
- **Type:** Primary theory + prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the coupling of market liquidity to funding liquidity; destabilising margins;
  liquidity spirals; and the specific predictions that speculators' capital and volatility act as
  *state variables* for market liquidity and risk premia, with a nonlinear effect when capital is
  already low.
- **Caveats:** a stylised model; the empirical margins evidence is historical (1987/1990/1998/2007
  crisis episodes) and documented largely from anecdote and public margin schedules.
- **Verification:** Oxford Academic landing page (volume, issue, pages, DOI) and author PDF.

### S-010 — Andersen, L., Duffie, D. and Song, Y. (2019)
"Funding Value Adjustments." *The Journal of Finance* 74(1): 145–192.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12739
(NBER working paper version: https://www.nber.org/papers/w23680)
- **Type:** Primary theory.
- **Supports:** funding value adjustments (FVAs) and margin value adjustments (MVAs) are debt-overhang
  costs to a dealer's shareholders; dealer quotations therefore embed funding costs; this creates a
  measurable, structural wedge between fundamental value and quoted price, and it explains why
  covered-interest-parity violations persist.
- **Caveats:** the paper analyses OTC dealer balance sheets; it is a valuation-theory result, not
  evidence that any particular quoted spread is mispriced.
- **Verification:** RePEc record (journal, volume, issue, pages, DOI) plus the NBER working-paper page.

### S-011 — Cont, R., Kukanov, A. and Stoikov, S. (2014)
"The Price Impact of Order Book Events." *Journal of Financial Econometrics* 12(1): 47–88.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/jjfinec/nbt003
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported) + primary modelling.
- **Supports:** short-horizon price changes are driven mainly by order-flow imbalance (OFI), with a
  roughly linear impact relation whose slope is inversely proportional to market depth; a square-root
  volume/price-impact relation follows from a scaling argument but is noisier.
- **Caveats:** 50 US stocks on NYSE TAQ data over a specific period; impact coefficients are
  state-dependent and the paper does not model intermediary balance-sheet capacity.
- **Verification:** Oxford Academic landing page (volume, issue, pages, DOI) and the DOI record.

### S-012 — Carr, P. and Wu, L. (2009)
"Variance Risk Premia." *The Review of Financial Studies* 22(3): 1315–1341.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhn038
- **Type:** Primary method + prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the variance-swap rate is a near-exact model-free synthesis of option prices and
  approximates the risk-neutral expected variance; realized minus implied variance is the variance
  risk premium; the premium is strongly negative for US equity indices and individual stocks.
- **Caveats:** the authors' own sample of five indices and 35 stocks; the synthesis is an
  approximation with a small model error.
- **Verification:** Oxford Academic DOI record and the NYU-hosted article PDF (abstract, results).

### S-013 — Bollerslev, T., Tauchen, G. and Zhou, H. (2009)
"Expected Stock Returns and Variance Risk Premia." *The Review of Financial Studies* 22(11):
4463–4492.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhp008
- **Type:** Primary theory + prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the variance risk premium is a state variable for aggregate equity returns, with the
  strongest predictive power at a quarterly horizon; the premium is large relative to other common
  predictors; the result requires model-free implied variance and high-frequency realized variation.
- **Caveats:** the authors note the predictive power of other predictors has diminished over time;
  the 1990–2007 sample has limited crisis episodes.
- **Verification:** Oxford Academic landing page (volume, issue, pages, DOI) and the author PDF.

### S-014 — Bakshi, G., Kapadia, N. and Madan, D. B. (2003)
"Stock Return Characteristics, Skew Laws, and the Differential Pricing of Individual Equity
Options." *The Review of Financial Studies* 16(1): 101–143.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/16.1.0101
- **Type:** Primary theory + prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the negative skew of individual equity returns and the systematic pricing of
  individual options at higher implied volatility than index options, consistent with investor
  demand for insurance-like payoffs at the single-name level.
- **Caveats:** a model with hedging-demand primitives; the empirical single-name option results
  predate the post-2010 explosion in single-name option volume and zero-days-to-expiration trading.
- **Verification:** DOI and bibliographic details confirmed from the reference list of a retrieved
  peer-reviewed article; abstract content corroborated by multiple independent citing records.

### S-015 — Santa-Clara, P. and Yan, S. (2010)
"Crashes, Volatility, and the Equity Premium: Lessons from S&P 500 Options." *The Review of
Economics and Statistics* 92(2): 435–451.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1162/rest.2010.11549
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the equity risk premium is substantially higher in high-crash-risk states than in
  low-crash-risk states, and option-implied crash measures predict the *state* rather than a
  static premium — evidence that crash-linked risk is priced on average and rewarded asymmetrically.
- **Caveats:** the premium is a time-average of very fat-tailed states; a naive harvesting strategy
  is exposed to the same crash states that generate the premium.
- **Verification:** DOI and bibliographic details confirmed from the reference list of a retrieved
  peer-reviewed article (Kell & Jiang, SSRN 2019482).

### S-016 — Barro, R. J. (2006)
"Rare Disasters and Asset Prices in the Twentieth Century." *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*
121(3): 823–866.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.121.3.823
- **Type:** Primary theory + prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the disaster-insurance framing of asset pricing — investors pay an insurance premium
  for protection against rare disasters, which is the canonical theoretical basis for a
  negatively-skewed risk premium and for the demand that creates it.
- **Caveats:** calibrated to a specific disaster-proxy data set; the insurance-price interpretation
  of a measured premium is a model-based inference, not a direct observation.
- **Verification:** DOI and bibliographic details confirmed from the reference list of a retrieved
  peer-reviewed article (Kell & Jiang, SSRN 2019482).

### S-017 — Acharya, V. V., Shin, H. S. and Yorulmazer, T. (2009)
"A Theory of Slow-Moving Capital and Contagion." (CEPR Discussion Paper 7147; NBER Finance WP
FIN-08-029; SSRN 1331610.)
DOI: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.1331610
- **Type:** Primary theory.
- **Supports:** equilibrium with slow-moving capital — arbitrage capital only moves when
  fire-sale discounts are deep enough, producing occasional fire sales, limited stand-by capital,
  and contagion across fundamentally unrelated assets that draw on a common pool of arbitrage
  capital.
- **Caveats:** a stylised equilibrium model; the "stand-by capital only moves when discounts are deep"
  result is a theorem under specific assumptions, not a measured parameter.
- **Verification:** CEPR publication page (title, authors, date, abstract), SSRN record, and the NYU
  Faculty Digital Archive entry (FIN-08-029).

### S-018 — Pedersen, L. H., Mitchell, M. L. and Pulvino, T. C. (2007)
"Slow Moving Capital." *American Economic Review* 97(2): 215–218.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.97.2.215
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the empirical fact-pattern that institutional capital moves slowly, so arbitrage
  opportunities persist and price pressures unwind only gradually.
- **Caveats:** a short AER Papers & Proceedings article; a fact-pattern rather than a full model.
- **Verification:** DOI and bibliographic details confirmed from the reference list of a retrieved
  peer-reviewed article (Acharya, Shin & Yorulmazer 2009).

### S-019 — Duffie, D. (2010)
"Presidential Address: Asset Price Dynamics with Slow-Moving Capital." *The Journal of Finance*
65(4): 1237–1267.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01569.x
- **Type:** Primary theory.
- **Supports:** the canonical framework for prices with slow-moving capital — the price impact of
  a flow shock decays over time, the decay is governed by how fast capital reallocates, and
  deviations from fundamentals can persist for long horizons.
- **Caveats:** a survey/presidential address rather than a single hypothesis test.
- **Verification:** DOI and bibliographic details confirmed from the reference list of a retrieved
  peer-reviewed article.

### S-020 — He, Z. and Xiong, W. (2012)
"Delegated asset management, investment mandates, and capital immobility." *Journal of Financial
Economics* 107(2): 239–258.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.08.010
- **Type:** Primary theory.
- **Supports:** investment mandates and career concerns make delegated capital immobile; managers
  trade toward their benchmark-relative objectives, producing flow-driven price pressure and
  comovement.
- **Caveats:** theory; the empirical content is the mechanism, not a test of any single strategy.
- **Verification:** DOI and bibliographic details confirmed from the reference list of a retrieved

### S-021 — Malliaris, S. G. and Yan, H. (2021)
"Reputation Concerns and Slow-Moving Capital." *The Review of Asset Pricing Studies* 11(1).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/rapstu/raab006
- **Type:** Primary theory.
- **Supports:** an explicit statement that capital is sometimes slow (leaving opportunities
  unexploited) and sometimes fast (relocating abruptly and moving prices with no fundamental news),
  with the switch driven by managers' reputation incentives.
- **Caveats:** theory; the "state-dependent speed of capital" is a model outcome, not a measured

- **Verification:** Oxford Academic landing page (title, authors, abstract, date, DOI).

### S-022 — Kondor, G. (2009)
"Risk in Dynamic Arbitrage: The Price Effects of Convergence Trading." *The Journal of Finance*
64(2): 631–655.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01445.x
- **Type:** Primary theory.
- **Supports:** convergence-trading strategies are exposed to a crowding risk factor: as more capital
  runs the same convergence, the price effects that generate the profit shrink and the risk of
  simultaneous exit rises.
- **Caveats:** theory; the crowding factor is a model construct, not an observable index.
- **Verification:** DOI and bibliographic details confirmed from the reference list of a retrieved
  peer-reviewed article (Malliaris & Yan 2021).

### S-023 — Abreu, A. and Brunnermeier, M. K. (2002)
"Synchronization risk and delayed arbitrage." *Journal of Financial Economics* 66(2–3): 341–360.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/s0304-405x(02)00227-1
- **Type:** Primary theory.
- **Supports:** arbitragers who fail to act in time are forced into synchronised trades later,
  which raises the price of liquidity and amplifies deviations; the delay in arbitrage is
  self-reinforcing.
- **Caveats:** theory; the amplification result is model-dependent.
- **Verification:** DOI and bibliographic details confirmed from the reference list of a retrieved
  peer-reviewed article (Acharya, Shin & Yorulmazer 2009).

### S-024 — Vayanos, D. and Woolley, P. (2013)
"An Institutional Theory of Momentum and Reversal." *The Review of Financial Studies* 26(5):
1087–1145.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hht014
- **Type:** Primary theory + prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** flows between funds generate momentum (through flow inertia and rational
  underreaction to expected future flows) and reversal (through flows pushing prices away from
  fundamental value) — the same institutional flow can produce both signs depending on the state.
- **Caveats:** a calibrated model; the Sharpe ratios quoted for momentum and value in the paper are
  model outputs, not measured performance.
- **Verification:** Oxford Academic landing page and the LSE Research Online record (volume, issue,
  pages, DOI).

### S-025 — Mitchell, M. L. and Pulvino, T. C. (2001)
"Characteristics of Risk and Return in Risk Arbitrage." *The Journal of Finance* 56(6):
2135–2175.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00401
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the risk-arbitrageur population (merger-spread positions) is a measurable, sizeable
  agent whose capital is committed to convergence positions and who therefore has a systematic
  exposure to the continuation (not resolution) of the spread.
- **Caveats:** pre-2010 sample; today's merger-arb and index-arb capital is far larger and more
  heterogeneous than the 1990s population studied.
- **Verification:** DOI and bibliographic details confirmed from the reference list of a retrieved
  peer-reviewed article.

### S-026 — Brown, G. W., Howard, P. D. and Lundblad, C. T. (2021)
"Crowded Trades and Tail Risk." *The Review of Financial Studies* 34(7).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhab107
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** security-level *crowdedness* can be measured from hedge fund holdings; the
  high-minus-low crowdedness spread is distinct from standard risk factors; exposure to crowdedness
  is associated with larger drawdowns during industry distress.
- **Caveats:** the crowding measure is built from 13F-style holdings with reporting lags (up to a
  quarter), so it is a slow, stale state variable; results are on hedge fund positions, not on all
  arbitrage capital.
- **Verification:** Oxford Academic landing page (title, authors, abstract, date, DOI).

### S-027 — Da, Z., Engelberg, J. and Gao, P. (2011)
"In Search of Attention." *The Journal of Finance* 66(5).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2011.01679.x
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** a direct, timestamped measure of investor attention (Google search volume); an
  attention increase predicts higher prices over the next two weeks and an eventual reversal within
  the year; the attention proxy is distinct from volume-based attention measures.
- **Caveats:** Russell 3000 sample 2004–2008; the Google search index is a retail-attention proxy and
  its coverage and stability have changed since 2010.
- **Verification:** Wiley landing page (title, authors, abstract, date) and the author-hosted PDF.

### S-028 — Lou, D., Polk, C. and Skouras, S. (2019)
"A tug of war: Overnight versus intraday expected returns." *Journal of Financial Economics*
134(1): 192–213.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.03.011
- **Type:** Prior empirical result (author-reported).
- **Supports:** the decomposition of strategy premia into overnight and intraday components, and the
  finding that the *spread* between the two components forecasts time variation in the strategy's
  close-to-close performance — i.e., the clientele composition, not the signal, drives when a
  known anomaly is harvestable.
- **Caveats:** the authors note that transaction costs reduce the attractiveness of the raw
  overnight/intraday patterns; the t-statistics reported are the authors' own.
- **Verification:** ScienceDirect landing page, LSE Research Online record (volume, issue, pages,
  DOI).

### S-029 — S&P Dow Jones Indices (venue documentation)
"S&P GSCI Methodology" (contract selection, contract weights, roll schedule, market-disruption
adjustment).
URL: https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/methodology-sp-gsci.pdf
- **Type:** Venue documentation (primary rules).
- **Supports:** the institutional clock the mechanism depends on — the S&P GSCI Spot Index rolls
  monthly from the **5th to the 9th business day**, in per-day proportions of 80/20, 60/40,
  40/60, 20/80 and 0/100 between the roll-out and roll-in contracts; a documented rule for
  *holding* roll weights if a market disruption event occurs; and the existence of sibling
  families with different conventions (S&P GSCI Pre-Roll, rolling 1st–5th business day; S&P GSCI
  Enhanced, with a partly dynamic, commodity-specific schedule).
- **Caveats:** the provider revises the methodology at its discretion, and the version in force on
  the event date governs; the Enhanced family is not the benchmark assumed in the candidate.
- **Verification:** document text read from the provider-hosted PDF through a text extraction of
  that exact URL (roll-weight tables and the page marker "S&P Dow Jones Indices: S&P GSCI
  Methodology 13"). A direct fetch of the PDF was refused by the publisher with HTTP 403 (bot
  protection); the identical roll schedule was cross-checked against the provider's "S&P GSCI
  Reference Guide" PDF (https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/methodology-sp-gsci-quick-guide.pdf),
  retrieved from the same domain.

### S-030 — Commodity Futures Trading Commission (regulator documentation)
"Commitments of Traders: Explanatory Notes" (Explanatory Notes for the COT reports, including the
Supplemental report and the "Index Traders" category).
URL: https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/ExplanatoryNotes/index.htm
- **Type:** Venue documentation (regulator's own definitions).
- **Supports:** the participant, the constraint and the flow, stated by the regulator: the
  Supplemental report "shows an additional category of 'Index Traders' in selected agricultural
  markets", whose members are "generally replicating a commodity index by establishing long
  futures positions in the component markets and then rolling those positions forward from future
  to future using a fixed methodology". The page also states the contamination caveats — the
  category "will include some long and short positions where traders have multi-dimensional trading
  activities" and "will not include some traders who are engaged in index trading" — the
  reporting-threshold convention (reportable positions normally cover 70–90% of total open
  interest), the "Percent of Open Interest" definition, and the crop-year aggregation convention.
- **Caveats:** the Index Traders category exists only in the Supplemental report and, historically,
  only in selected agricultural markets; the definitional caveats above are material for any state
  variable built on it.
- **Verification:** page fetched and read directly; the quoted passages are verbatim from the page.

### S-032 — Bessembinder, H., Carrion, A., Tuttle, L. and Venkataraman, K. (2016)
"Liquidity, resiliency and market quality around predictable trades: Theory and evidence."
*Journal of Financial Economics* 121(1): 142–166.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2016.02.011
- **Type:** Primary theory + prior empirical result (author-reported).

## Source gaps (explicitly unresolved)

Gap status is reviewed every cycle. Cycle 2 closed or narrowed G-01, G-02, G-04 and G-05, and
could not close G-03. Verdicts are recorded here so the negative results survive.

- **G-01 — public funding-state proxy for dealer funding value adjustments.** **NARROWED, not
  closed; verdict: viable only with a specified composite.** Cycle 2 verified four public,
  regularly published ingredients that no Cycle-1 source had established: daily SOFR and the
  related GC repo rates (S-041), daily standing-repo-facility take-up and awarded rates (S-042),
  and a recurring public balance-sheet disclosure of the primary dealers (S-042). What is still
  missing is the *target* of the proxy — a published, continuous series of dealer funding value
  adjustments — and, therefore, any validated mapping from the public composite to disclosed FVAs.
  IDEA-002 must therefore specify a composite (a SOFR-to-IOER or SOFR-to-Treasury spread, plus
  standing-repo stress, plus a dealer-balance-sheet component) and a validation design against
  dealer disclosures, and it must treat the composite as a *state indicator*, not as the FVA
  itself. If the validation against disclosed FVAs cannot be performed, IDEA-002 stays parked
  (P-004) rather than being re-specified on a weaker proxy.
- **G-02 — size of index-linked assets tracking a benchmark.** **NARROWED; verdict: a
  category-level public proxy exists, a benchmark-specific series does not.** Cycle 2 verified the
  ICI's public monthly series for total fund assets and for the active-versus-index split
  (S-040), which supports a *scale* variable for passive assets at the level of "the size of the
  indexed complex", with the explicit exclusions the ICI itself states (ETFs and
  funds-of-funds are excluded from the headline totals) and with the limitation that it is
  category-level, not tied to any one benchmark. Separately, in the futures market there is a
  *direct* measure of the passive complex at contract level — the CFTC "Index Traders" positions
  (S-030) — which is the model a benchmark-specific series would have to emulate. The
  benchmark-specific series remains open as **G-07**.
- **G-03 — current index-fund trading convention at the effective close.** **NOT CLOSED;
  re-framed into an answerable empirical question.** No source was found that establishes whether
  index funds trade at the effective close or pre-position, and none was found in Cycle 2 either.
  What Cycle 2 can do is narrow the question: the *testable* object is the **concentration of
  passive flow in the closing auction and whether that concentration has changed**, which is
  measurable from exchange-published closing-auction volume statistics. Those statistics were
  **not** located or verified this cycle, so the narrowed form stays open as **G-08**, and IDEA-001
  must carry the premise as an explicit assumption to be checked rather than as a documented fact.
- **G-04 — timely crowd-depth proxy.** **PARTIALLY CLOSED for the futures domain.** The CFTC's
  weekly "Index Traders" and Disaggregated COT reports (S-030, S-031) provide a public,
  contract-level, dated measure of both the passive position and the arbitrage/liquidity-supply
  position — the exact pair of state variables IDEA-005 needs, with a known five-day publication
  lag (S-037). For **equities** the gap is unchanged: the only measure located remains the
  quarterly-stale 13F-based crowding measure (S-026), and IDEA-004's proxy-validity kill
  condition stands.
- **G-05 — post-2010 single-name option market structure.** **PARTIALLY CLOSED.** Cycle 2
  characterised the structural change using the exchange's own published research and statistics
  (S-043): same-day expiries now account for the majority of index option volume (about 5% of
  SPX options volume in 2016 to 59–62% in 2025), the flow is estimated to be roughly half retail
  and about 98% electronically traded, and the exchange's own estimate is that net market-maker
  gamma is de minimis. What remains unsettled is the *direction and size of the effect on the
  single-name insurance-demand channel* of S-014, because single-name 0DTE and single-name retail
  statistics were not established, and because the structural change is documented by an
  interested party rather than in peer-reviewed work. IDEA-003 must therefore treat the
  single-name version of the channel as an explicitly weaker, separately-validated extension.
- **G-06 (new) — the commodity basis-convergence literature, and any published work that already
  conditions a futures spread on a positioning state.** Not retrieved in Cycle 2. This is a
  blocking check for IDEA-005's originality: if the concession around the roll is simply a
  convergence-to-carry trade with a calendar catalyst, or if the cross-sectional state condition
  is already published, IDEA-005 must be reclassified as a variant of a known baseline. Must be
  closed before IDEA-005 is presented as a discovery.
- **G-07 (new) — a benchmark-specific time series of index-linked assets** (for example, assets
  tracking the S&P 500 specifically, rather than the aggregate indexed complex). No verified
  source was found. The SPIVA scorecards (S-040 and the related S&P DJI publications) were
  inspected and the parts retrieved did not publish index-fund assets by benchmark; the SPIVA

  precondition for the flow-scaling test in IDEA-001 (V-001) and for the scale variable in
  IDEA-005.
- **G-08 (new) — public closing-auction volume statistics** (the data source that would allow
